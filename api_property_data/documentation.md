# Domain API: Properties & Locations

Domain data provides a near-real-time view of how Australia’s property market is advertised, searched, transacted and described at both property and area level. Domain is one of Australia’s major property platforms, and its data reflects activity across residential, commercial and business listings, real estate agencies and agents, new development projects, property records, suburb performance indicators, sales results and location profiles. For researchers who are new to the data, it is useful to think of Domain as a source of market-facing property intelligence: it captures what is listed, where it is listed, who is advertising it, how places are described, and how property markets are performing across suburbs and cities.

Through AURIN, eligible researchers can access selected Domain API products for applied research, academic publication and internal non-commercial proofs-of-concept. The data is accessed programmatically through the AURIN Data Provider platform rather than as a bulk download, meaning each request retrieves current API responses at the time it is made. Access is subject to application, approval and acceptance of the relevant end-user agreement, and a monthly call limit applies across the available Domain APIs. This access model is designed for structured, query-based research workflows where users retrieve the specific locations, time periods, property types or records needed for their analysis.

Because Domain uses its own property, suburb, region and location identifiers, the data does not map directly onto ABS statistical geographies by default. AURIN therefore provides correspondence tables to help researchers connect Domain suburbs and regions to standard Australian geographies such as GCCSA, SA2, SA3 and SA4 boundaries. These lookup files are important for spatial analysis, comparison with Census or other AURIN datasets, and aggregation of Domain-derived indicators into recognised planning and research geographies.

## Group 2: Properties & Locations

Group 2, Properties & Locations, is the part of the Domain API that gives researchers property-level records and suburb-level market intelligence across Australia. It brings together detailed property records, sales and listing history, price and rent estimates, suburb performance statistics, Census demographic profiles, weekly auction clearance results and aggregate location profiles. For users new to Domain data, this group is best understood as a way to move between individual property evidence and broader area-level indicators of housing market conditions. The property record endpoints support lookups by Domain property ID and street-address resolution through addressLocators, allowing researchers to retrieve a current property snapshot alongside available sales history, listing history, estimates and images. The area-level endpoints support suburb, postcode, state and Domain location-ID workflows, making the group useful for questions about suburb performance, market change, demographic context and local property availability. Suburb performance statistics are returned by quarter over a rolling roughly ten-year window, while Census demographics are tied to the 2011, 2016 and 2021 release years. This API group is particularly useful for studies that need to connect property-market activity with place-based analysis. Researchers can examine changes in median prices, rents, days on market, rental yields and other suburb indicators; compare demographic profiles with market outcomes; retrieve current weekly auction results by city; or use location profiles as high-level summaries of suburb characteristics. The accompanying training materials, especially notebook-2-suburb-statistics and notebook-4-spatial-querying, support time-series suburb analysis and spatial querying workflows. There are several important constraints to account for when designing queries. Suburb performance statistics are capped at 45 periods, and House and Unit statistics must be queried separately. Property records and location profiles return one record per call, so larger workflows need to be planned around the monthly credit allocation. Auction results represent the current week only and should not be treated as a historical archive. Demographic endpoints are useful for single-suburb lookups, but ABS sources may be more efficient for broad multi-suburb demographic extraction. The Group 2 endpoint reference in metadata/metadata.md documents the available parameters, response fields, limits and required disclaimers.
